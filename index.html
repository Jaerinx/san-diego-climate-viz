Ninety Years of Familiar Weather

San Diego's climate has been defined by consistency. The same mild winters, dry summers, and coastal marine layer you see in 1925 appear again in 2014. That stability is real.

But a quiet signal persists. 13 of the 15 years since 2000 ran warmer than the mid-century average, and the recent decade sits about 0.34°C above the 90-year mean: small, but steady.

Precipitation tells a wilder story. San Diego's rainfall is defined by its extremes. In 1959, the city received 766 mm of rain. The very next year: 222 mm. A 544 mm swing in twelve months. Twenty-five of the 90 years topped 400 mm; only three fell below 150 mm. The "average" San Diego year is actually rare, and climate projections suggest the swings intensify as warming accelerates.
