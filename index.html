Prototype

1. Warming trend in annual mean temperature

Annual temperatures (thin line) with a 5-year moving average (bold) showing a gradual warming trend over nine decades.

2. Temperature anomalies from a 1961–1990 baseline

Each bar shows how much the annual mean departed from the 1961–1990 baseline, a standard reference period for tracking warming.

3. Modeled sea level near the San Diego coast

Modeled sea surface height at the nearest ocean grid point, relative to the 1961–1990 average in millimeters.

4. Year-to-year precipitation totals

Annual rainfall totals with a classic wet-winter, dry-summer pattern. Dashed line marks the 1961–1990 average.

5. San Diego County population density

Population density using Census counts (red dots) and a fixed land area of 4,207 sq mi, with linear interpolation between census years.

Write-Up

We built "San Diego Climate Over the Last Century," an interactive D3.js visualization using NOAA CMIP6 historical simulation data covering annual mean temperature, temperature anomalies, sea surface height, and precipitation near San Diego. We also pulled U.S. Census data for county population density from 1925 to 2014. The page is structured as a scrollable story, walking readers through warming trends, anomalies, sea level, rainfall, and population in sequence.

The biggest design challenge ahead is weaving the five plots into one coherent story. Right now each chart stands on its own, so we will need to balance interactivity and annotation to avoid overplotting. Presenting sea level and precipitation data without misleading readers will also require careful framing since the numbers come from CMIP6 model simulations, not direct observations. Layering in the population data will add meaningful context about what these climate shifts actually mean for people living in San Diego.

Data: NOAA Public CMIP6 on Google Cloud. Course context: DSC 106 Project 3. CMIP6 values are model simulations. Population data are from the U.S. Census, not CMIP6.
